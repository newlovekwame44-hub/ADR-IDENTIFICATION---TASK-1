ADR Identification Basics: Task 1

Case summary
* Patient: Female, 45 years
* Presenting symptoms: Rash, itching, nausea, dizziness are presented as symptoms
* Medication history: Amoxicillin (started 5 days ago, suspect drug); paracetamol as required (concomitant)
* Timelines: Day -5: amoxicillin was started, Day -2 symptoms started, Day 0 symptoms are ongoing

 Assessment
Symptoms began about 3 days after starting the amoxicillin, a delayed-type penicillin hypersensitivity reaction. Amoxicillin ADRs include rash and pruritus, which are well documented, as well as nausea, a common GI side effect. It's very unlikely that paracetamol was responsible – it's only taken when needed, and rash from paracetamol is uncommon. Early systemic allergy should be considered in the case of dizziness.

Suspected drug: Amoxicillin
Typical reaction: Itching (urticaria) with rash

Scale: 9 (definite), 5-8 (probable), 1-4 (possible), 0 or less (doubtful).

WHO-UMC: Possible. This becomes Probable/Likely if there is a positive dechallenge and no other cause.

Differential diagnoses
* Viral exanthem
* The treatment or cure for the underlying infection.
* Other new medications, foods or supplements

 Management plan
1. Assess the severity (airway, angioedema, blisters, mucosal, fever) and take vital signs.
2. Discontinue amoxicillin and use an alternative antibiotic (except one that is a beta-lactam).
3. Symptomatic treatment (antihistamine) and watch for improvement.
4. Record on the patient's chart that the patient may be allergic to penicillin.
5. Report to the Ghana FDA using the ADR reporting form
